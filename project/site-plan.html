🌍 Site Name

Zimbabwe Wildlife Conservation Network

Why this name was selected: This name clearly communicates the website's focus on wildlife conservation specifically in Zimbabwe. It establishes the site as a hub for conservation information, connecting visitors with Zimbabwe's unique wildlife heritage and preservation efforts. The name is descriptive, memorable, and immediately conveys the site's purpose to potential visitors.

Domain (for reference): zimbabwe-wildlife-conservation.org

🎯 Site Purpose

The Zimbabwe Wildlife Conservation Network website serves as an educational and engagement hub for wildlife conservation in Zimbabwe. The site provides:

  • Information about Zimbabwe's national parks and wildlife reserves (Hwange, Mana Pools, Gonarezhou, Matobo Hills)
  • Profiles of endangered and native wildlife species (elephants, rhinos, lions, and African wild dogs)
  • Conservation success stories and current challenges
  • Ways for visitors to get involved through donations, volunteer opportunities, and wildlife adoption programs
  • Integration of live weather data for major parks using OpenWeatherMap API
  • A directory of partner organizations and conservation projects

❓ User Scenarios

These questions represent the site's target audience — wildlife enthusiasts, donors, and researchers — and drive the content of each page.

Scenario 1 — The Wildlife Photographer

"Which national park in Zimbabwe offers the best chance to see elephants, and what is the weather like there right now?"

Answer the site provides: Species profiles with habitat data, park pages with live weather from the OpenWeatherMap API, and a 3-day forecast for each location.

Scenario 2 — The Prospective Donor

"How can I donate to help protect rhinos from poaching, and how will my money be used?"

Answer the site provides: A "Get Involved" page with donation options, an adoption program, and transparency reports showing how funds are allocated.

Scenario 3 — The University Student

"What are the current population numbers for African wild dogs in Zimbabwe, and what conservation efforts are underway?"

Answer the site provides: Species profiles with population data, conservation status, and links to ongoing research and partner organizations.

🎨 Color Scheme

The palette draws from the natural tones of the Zimbabwean savanna — deep greens for vegetation, amber for the sun and soil, and a warm off-white background for readability.

Dark Green #1B5E20

Headings, header, footer

Medium Green #2E7D32

Navigation, section borders

Amber #FF8F00

Buttons, accents, highlights

Warm Off-White #F5F5F0

Page background

Dark Gray #2C2C2C

Body text

Usage Summary

  • #1B5E20 Dark Green — all headings (h1–h6), header background, footer background
  • #2E7D32 Medium Green — navigation bar, card left borders, hover states
  • #FF8F00 Amber — call-to-action buttons, section accents, link highlights
  • #F5F5F0 Warm Off-White — page background color
  • #2C2C2C Dark Gray — body text throughout the site

✍️ Typography

Two Google Fonts are used: one for headings (Montserrat) and one for body text (Open Sans). Both are highly legible on screens and pair well together.

Montserrat — Headings

The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog. 1234567890

Where used: All headings (h1–h6), navigation links, button labels. The bold geometric forms give structure and hierarchy to the page.

Open Sans — Body Text

The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog. 1234567890

Where used: Paragraphs, list items, form labels, captions, and other long-form reading text. Its open forms and generous spacing make it comfortable for extended reading.
